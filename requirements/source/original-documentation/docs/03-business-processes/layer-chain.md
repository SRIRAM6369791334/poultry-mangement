# Layer Business Chain (End-to-End Process)

## 1. Introduction
The Layer Business Chain describes the end-to-end workflow of a commercial egg production enterprise, from setting up the infrastructure to selling eggs and finally depleting the flock. This process map ensures the ERP captures all operational and financial touchpoints.

## 2. The Value Chain

### Phase 1: Planning & Procurement
- **Production Planning:** Determining market demand and scheduling flock placements to ensure consistent egg supply.
- **House Preparation:** Cleaning, disinfecting, and preparing the housing system (cage, barn, free-range).
- **Pullet Procurement:** 
  - *Option A (Integrated):* Hatchery transfers day-old chicks to rearing houses.
  - *Option B (Commercial):* Purchasing 16-week-old point-of-lay pullets from a rearing farm.
- **Feed & Supply Procurement:** Establishing purchase orders for vaccines, equipment, and phase-specific layer feed.

### Phase 2: Rearing (If applicable)
- **Brooding:** Day 0 to 4 weeks. High temperature, starter feed.
- **Growing:** 4 to 16 weeks. Step-down lighting, body weight uniformity checks.
- **Transfer:** Moving birds to the laying facility. Financial transfer of accumulated pullet costs to the layer farm as an asset.

### Phase 3: Egg Production
- **Onset of Lay:** Birds acclimatize to layer houses. Light stimulation begins. First eggs (usually small/peewee) are produced.
- **Daily Routine:**
  - Morning checks: Temperature, water flow, feed distribution.
  - Egg Collection: Automated belts or manual collection.
  - Data Entry: Recording mortality, feed intake, and egg counts.
- **Peak Production:** 25-35 weeks of age. Focus on nutrition and maintaining body weight.
- **Late Production:** Focus on shell quality and managing egg weight.

### Phase 4: Egg Processing & Inventory
- **Transfer to Packing:** Eggs move from farm houses to the packing/grading facility.
- **Grading & Sorting:** Eggs are washed (in some regions), candled for defects (blood spots, cracks), and weighed.
- **Categorization:** Sorted into Jumbo, Extra Large, Large, Medium, Small, Peewee.
- **Packaging:** Packed into flats (trays of 30) or consumer cartons (dozens).
- **Cold Storage:** Stored in temperature-controlled rooms, logging expiry dates (FIFO).

### Phase 5: Sales & Distribution
- **Order Management:** Receiving orders from retail chains, wholesalers, or institutional buyers.
- **Pricing:** Dynamic pricing based on egg size, market rates, and contracts.
- **Dispatch:** Generating delivery notes, loading trucks, tracking dispatch logistics.
- **Invoicing & Receivables:** Billing clients and tracking payments.

### Phase 6: End of Cycle (Depletion)
- **Flock Evaluation:** At ~72-90 weeks, economic evaluation of feed cost vs. egg value.
- **Moulting (Optional):** [OPEN_RESEARCH_ITEM] Induced moulting to force a second laying cycle (varies by region/animal welfare laws).
- **Flock Sale/Depletion:** Selling the flock as spent hens (soup hens/processed meat).
- **Manure Management:** Selling or processing poultry manure/litter as fertilizer.
- **House Resting:** Cycle restarts.

## 3. Financial Integration Points
- **Capitalization:** Cost of rearing a pullet is capitalized and amortized over the laying cycle.
- **COGS (Cost of Goods Sold):** Feed is the primary cost driver (70%+). Egg production costs must include feed, amortized pullet cost, labor, overhead, and packaging.
- **Revenue Streams:** Graded eggs, liquid egg (if processing broken eggs), spent hens, manure.
