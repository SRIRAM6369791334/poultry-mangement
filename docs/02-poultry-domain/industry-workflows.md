# Industry Workflows
This document describes the standard operational workflows in the poultry industry that the ERP must support.

## 1. Daily Farm Routine (The "Morning to Evening" Loop)
- **Trigger**: Start of day (e.g., 6:00 AM)
- **Steps**:
  1. **Inspection**: Farm worker walks the house, checking environment (temperature, ventilation, litter condition).
  2. **Mortality Collection**: Dead birds are collected, counted, and disposed of. (Logged into ERP).
  3. **Culling**: Sick/lame birds are humanely euthanized. (Logged into ERP).
  4. **Feed & Water Check**: Silo levels checked, water lines flushed, pressure checked. Feed consumption recorded.
  5. **Egg Collection (Layers/Breeders)**: Usually done multiple times a day. Eggs graded and stored in cool room.
  6. **Data Entry**: Supervisor enters daily mortality, feed, water, temp, and egg counts into ERP.
  7. **Review**: Manager reviews daily dashboard for alerts (e.g., water/feed spikes).

## 2. Weekly Management Activities
- **Trigger**: Weekly schedule (e.g., every Tuesday)
- **Steps**:
  1. **Body Weight Sampling**: Random sample of birds caught and weighed to track against growth curve.
  2. **Feed Ordering**: Based on projected consumption for next week, order placed to Feed Mill.
  3. **Vaccination/Medication**: Administer scheduled water or spray vaccines (e.g., Newcastle Disease booster).
  4. **Vet Visit**: External or internal vet audits flock health and signs off on biosecurity.
  5. **Cost Review**: Weekly cost-of-production variance analysis by Finance.

## 3. Batch Lifecycle Management (Broiler)
- **Trigger**: Farm preparation requirement
- **Steps**:
  1. **Preparation**: Shed is washed, disinfected, rested (Downtime: 10-14 days).
  2. **Pre-Placement**: Wood shavings spread, heaters on, feed/water ready.
  3. **Placement (Day 0)**: Day Old Chicks (DOCs) arrive. Counted, sample weighed, placed.
  4. **Brooding Phase (Day 0-14)**: High heat, starter feed, intense monitoring.
  5. **Grow-out Phase (Day 15-30)**: Ventilation focus, grower feed.
  6. **Finisher Phase (Day 30-35/42)**: Maximize weight gain, withdrawal feed (no antibiotics).
  7. **Harvesting (Catching)**: Feed withdrawn 8-10 hours prior. Catching crew loads birds onto trucks.
  8. **Batch Closure**: All birds gone. Final weights received from processing plant. EPEF calculated.
  9. **Settlement**: Contract farmer paid based on performance.

## 4. Flock Lifecycle Management (Layers/Breeders)
- **Trigger**: Long-term investment cycle
- **Steps**:
  1. **Rearing/Pullet Phase (Week 0-16)**: Growing the bird, controlling weight and light to prevent early sexual maturity.
  2. **Transfer (Week 16-18)**: Moved from rearing house to laying house.
  3. **Pre-Lay (Week 18-20)**: Light stimulation begins. First eggs (pullet eggs).
  4. **Peak Production (Week 24-32)**: Hen-Day production hits 90-95%. Maximum feed intake.
  5. **Post-Peak (Week 33-70+)**: Gradual decline in lay rate, increase in egg size.
  6. **Molting (Optional)**: Induced stress to rest birds and start a second laying cycle.
  7. **Depopulation**: Spent hens sold for low-grade meat. House cleaned.

## 5. Feed Mill Operations (Integrated companies)
- **Trigger**: Feed demand from farms
- **Steps**:
  1. **Procurement**: Buying corn, soy, additives based on formulation needs.
  2. **Receiving & QA**: Testing incoming raw materials for moisture, toxins (aflatoxin).
  3. **Formulation**: Least-cost formulation by nutritionist.
  4. **Batching & Milling**: Grinding, mixing, pelleting.
  5. **Dispatch**: Loading finished feed onto auger trucks for delivery to specific farms.
  6. **Invoicing/Internal Transfer**: Charging the farm cost-center.

## Industry Performance Benchmarks (Reference)
*Data serves as baseline targets within the ERP for alerts.*
- **Broiler (Cobb 500 / Ross 308 at 35 days)**: Target weight ~2.1 - 2.3 kg, FCR ~1.45 - 1.55, Mortality < 4%.
- **Layer (Hy-Line Brown)**: Peak lay 96%, 350+ eggs per hen housed to 80 weeks, FCR 2.0 kg/kg egg mass.
- **Breeder (Ross 308)**: Peak hatchability > 88%, ~140 chicks/hen housed.
