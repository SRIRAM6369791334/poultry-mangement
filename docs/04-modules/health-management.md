# Health Management Module

## Purpose
The Health Management module is a critical component of the Poultry Management ERP, designed to track, manage, and prevent health issues across poultry flocks. It integrates disease tracking, vaccination scheduling, medication administration, biosecurity protocols, and veterinary record-keeping to ensure flock health and minimize economic losses.

## 1. Disease Catalog
*Source: Merck Veterinary Manual, WOAH (OIE), FAO*

### Viral Diseases
1. **Newcastle Disease (ND / Ranikhet)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Avian paramyxovirus serotype 1
   - **Symptoms**: Respiratory (gasping, coughing), nervous (tremors, paralyzed wings/legs), digestive (greenish diarrhea).
   - **Affected Birds**: All (broilers, layers, breeders).
   - **Mortality Rate**: Up to 100% in virulent strains.
   - **Treatment**: None (viral). Antibiotics for secondary bacterial infections.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination (live and inactivated). Biosecurity.
   - **Economic Impact**: High (trade restrictions, mortality, egg production drop).

2. **Infectious Bursal Disease (IBD / Gumboro)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Birnavirus
   - **Symptoms**: Depression, watery diarrhea, ruffled feathers, swollen bursa of Fabricius.
   - **Affected Birds**: Young birds (3-6 weeks).
   - **Mortality Rate**: 5-20% (up to 60% in highly virulent strains).
   - **Treatment**: None. Supportive care (vitamin/electrolyte supplements).
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination of breeders (maternal immunity) and chicks.
   - **Economic Impact**: High (immunosuppression leading to secondary infections).

3. **Marek's Disease**
   - **Causative Agent**: Alphaherpesvirus
   - **Symptoms**: Paralysis of legs/wings, tumors in internal organs, irregular pupil shape (grey eye).
   - **Affected Birds**: Chickens (usually 12-30 weeks).
   - **Mortality Rate**: Variable, can be high (up to 80%) if unvaccinated.
   - **Treatment**: None.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination (in-ovo or day 1 SC injection).
   - **Economic Impact**: High (condemnation at slaughter, mortality).

4. **Avian Influenza (AI / Bird Flu)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Orthomyxovirus (Type A)
   - **Symptoms**: Sudden death, cyanosis of comb/wattles, respiratory distress, drop in egg production.
   - **Affected Birds**: All poultry.
   - **Mortality Rate**: Up to 100% (HPAI).
   - **Treatment**: None. Stamping out (culling).
   - **Prevention**: Strict biosecurity. Vaccination (where permitted by authorities).
   - **Economic Impact**: Catastrophic (flock culling, export bans).

5. **Infectious Bronchitis (IB)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Coronavirus
   - **Symptoms**: Sneezing, gasping, wet eyes, misshapen eggs with poor shell quality.
   - **Affected Birds**: Chickens (all ages).
   - **Mortality Rate**: Low (unless secondary infections occur).
   - **Treatment**: Supportive care, antibiotics for secondary infections.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination (live and killed).
   - **Economic Impact**: High (drop in egg production and quality).

6. **Egg Drop Syndrome (EDS 76)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Adenovirus
   - **Symptoms**: Pale, soft-shelled, or shell-less eggs.
   - **Affected Birds**: Layers and breeders (peak production).
   - **Mortality Rate**: Negligible.
   - **Treatment**: None.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination before point of lay.
   - **Economic Impact**: Moderate (loss of marketable eggs).

7. **Fowl Pox**
   - **Causative Agent**: Avipoxvirus
   - **Symptoms**: Warty nodules on unfeathered areas (dry pox), diphtheritic membranes in mouth/trachea (wet pox).
   - **Affected Birds**: All poultry.
   - **Mortality Rate**: Low (dry pox), Moderate (wet pox).
   - **Treatment**: None.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination (wing web). Vector control (mosquitoes).
   - **Economic Impact**: Moderate (growth retardation, production drop).

8. **Avian Encephalomyelitis (AE)**
   - **Causative Agent**: Tremovirus
   - **Symptoms**: Tremors of head and neck, ataxia, paralysis.
   - **Affected Birds**: Young chicks (1-3 weeks), temporary egg drop in adults.
   - **Mortality Rate**: Up to 25% in chicks.
   - **Treatment**: None.
   - **Prevention**: Vaccination of breeders to provide maternal antibodies.
   - **Economic Impact**: Moderate.

### Bacterial Diseases
9. **Colibacillosis (E. coli)**
   - **Causative Agent**: *Escherichia coli*
   - **Symptoms**: Respiratory distress, omphalitis (yolk sac infection), peritonitis, septicemia.
   - **Affected Birds**: All.
   - **Mortality Rate**: 5-20%.
   - **Treatment**: Antibiotics (based on sensitivity test).
   - **Prevention**: Sanitation, water treatment, reducing stress.
   - **Economic Impact**: High (condemnations, mortality).

10. **Salmonellosis (Fowl Typhoid / Pullorum Disease)**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Salmonella* Gallinarum / Pullorum
    - **Symptoms**: White diarrhea, huddling, high mortality in chicks, drop in egg production.
    - **Affected Birds**: All.
    - **Mortality Rate**: High in young birds.
    - **Treatment**: Antibiotics (may not eliminate carrier state).
    - **Prevention**: Eradication programs, strict biosecurity, competitive exclusion.
    - **Economic Impact**: High (zoonotic importance, eradication costs).

11. **Mycoplasmosis (CRD)**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Mycoplasma gallisepticum*
    - **Symptoms**: Tracheal rales, nasal discharge, coughing, facial swelling.
    - **Affected Birds**: All.
    - **Mortality Rate**: Low (but high morbidity).
    - **Treatment**: Antibiotics (Tylosin, Tetracyclines).
    - **Prevention**: Sourcing from MG-free breeder flocks, biosecurity.
    - **Economic Impact**: High (feed conversion loss, carcass condemnation).

12. **Fowl Cholera**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Pasteurella multocida*
    - **Symptoms**: Sudden death, swollen wattles, greenish diarrhea, respiratory signs.
    - **Affected Birds**: Older chickens, turkeys.
    - **Mortality Rate**: High in acute outbreaks.
    - **Treatment**: Antibiotics (Sulfa drugs, Penicillin).
    - **Prevention**: Vaccination, rodent control.
    - **Economic Impact**: High (mortality).

13. **Necrotic Enteritis**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Clostridium perfringens*
    - **Symptoms**: Sudden death, severe depression, dark diarrhea, friable intestines.
    - **Affected Birds**: Broilers (2-5 weeks).
    - **Mortality Rate**: 5-50%.
    - **Treatment**: Antibiotics (Bacitracin, Lincomycin) in water.
    - **Prevention**: Coccidiosis control, pre/probiotics, managing diet changes.
    - **Economic Impact**: High (mortality, reduced FCR).

### Parasitic Diseases
14. **Coccidiosis**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Eimeria spp.*
    - **Symptoms**: Bloody diarrhea, unthriftiness, pale combs, huddling.
    - **Affected Birds**: Young birds.
    - **Mortality Rate**: Variable, can be high.
    - **Treatment**: Anticoccidials in water (Amprolium, Toltrazuril).
    - **Prevention**: Ionophores/chemicals in feed, live vaccines, litter management.
    - **Economic Impact**: Very High (major cost in broiler production).

15. **Worms (Ascariasis)**
    - **Causative Agent**: *Ascaridia galli* (Roundworms)
    - **Symptoms**: Weight loss, diarrhea, poor egg production.
    - **Affected Birds**: Birds on floor/free-range.
    - **Mortality Rate**: Low.
    - **Treatment**: Anthelmintics (Levamisole, Fenbendazole).
    - **Prevention**: Regular deworming, litter management.
    - **Economic Impact**: Moderate.

16. **External Parasites (Mites/Lice)**
    - **Causative Agent**: Red Mites, Northern Fowl Mites.
    - **Symptoms**: Anemia, restlessness, feather pecking, drop in production.
    - **Affected Birds**: Layers, Breeders.
    - **Mortality Rate**: Low (can cause mortality due to severe anemia).
    - **Treatment**: Acaricides (sprays, dusts).
    - **Prevention**: House cleaning, biosecurity.
    - **Economic Impact**: Moderate (reduced egg production, feed efficiency).

### Metabolic and Nutritional
17. **Fatty Liver Hemorrhagic Syndrome (FLHS)**
    - **Causative Agent**: Metabolic (excess calorie intake, low exercise).
    - **Symptoms**: Sudden death in overweight laying hens, pale combs, fatty liver with hemorrhages.
    - **Mortality Rate**: Moderate (sporadic).
    - **Prevention**: Diet formulation (Choline, Inositol, Vitamin E, managing energy).
    
18. **Cage Layer Fatigue**
    - **Causative Agent**: Calcium/Phosphorus imbalance or deficiency.
    - **Symptoms**: Paralysis in highly productive caged layers, fragile bones.
    - **Treatment**: Calcium supplementation.
    - **Prevention**: Proper pre-lay and lay calcium nutrition.

19. **Ascites (Pulmonary Hypertension Syndrome)**
    - **Causative Agent**: Metabolic (oxygen demand exceeds supply).
    - **Symptoms**: Fluid in abdomen, panting, cyanosis.
    - **Affected Birds**: Fast-growing broilers.
    - **Prevention**: Ventilation management, temperature control, feed restriction.

20. **Rickets**
    - **Causative Agent**: Vitamin D3, Ca, or P deficiency.
    - **Symptoms**: Soft, rubbery bones/beaks, lameness.
    - **Prevention**: Balanced diet formulation.

## 2. Vaccination Management

### Vaccine Types
- **Live Attenuated**: Induces strong cellular and humoral immunity. Risk of mild disease.
- **Killed / Inactivated**: Safer, requires individual injection, longer-lasting immunity. Usually requires a live primer.
- **Recombinant**: Vector vaccines (e.g., HVT expressing ND or IBD). Given in hatchery.

### Administration Routes
- Drinking water, Eye drop, Spray, Injection (Subcutaneous/Intramuscular), In-ovo (day 18 of incubation), Wing web.

### Standard Vaccination Schedules

#### Broilers (Short life cycle: 35-42 days)
| Age | Disease/Vaccine | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 (Hatchery) | Marek's (HVT), IB, ND | SC Injection / Spray |
| Day 10-14 | IBD (Gumboro) | Drinking Water / Eye drop |
| Day 18-21 | ND Booster (LaSota) | Drinking Water |
*Note: Recombinant in-ovo vaccines are increasingly replacing field vaccinations.*

#### Layers (Long life cycle: up to 80-100 weeks)
| Age | Disease/Vaccine | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Marek's, ND, IB | SC Injection / Spray |
| Week 2 | IBD (Gumboro) | Drinking Water |
| Week 3 | ND + IB | Drinking Water / Eye drop |
| Week 4 | IBD Booster | Drinking Water |
| Week 6 | Fowl Pox | Wing Web stab |
| Week 8 | ND + IB | Drinking Water |
| Week 12 | Avian Encephalomyelitis (AE) + Fowl Pox | Wing Web / Water |
| Week 14-16 | ND + IB + EDS (Inactivated) | IM / SC Injection |
| During Lay | ND + IB Live Boosters | Spray / Water (every 8-10 weeks) |

#### Breeders (Similar to layers, plus additional protection for progeny)
- Same schedule as layers, plus:
- **Week 10**: Reovirus, Cholera (depending on region).
- **Week 15-18**: Inactivated multivalent vaccine (ND, IB, IBD, Reo, EDS) via IM/SC to ensure high maternal antibodies for chicks.

### Cold Chain & Storage
- **Requirement (FR-HLT-01)**: Live vaccines must be stored at 2-8°C. Frozen vaccines (e.g., cell-associated Marek's) in liquid nitrogen (-196°C).
- **Validation**: System must record vaccine fridge temperatures and flag excursions.

### Vaccination Recording & Missed Vaccinations
- **Records (FR-HLT-02)**: Must capture Date, Batch ID, Vaccine Name, Manufacturer, Serial Number, Expiry, Route, Dose, and Operator.
- **Missed Handling**: Alert generated if a scheduled vaccination is missed by >24 hours. Rescheduling logic applied based on veterinary consultation.

## 3. Medication Management
*Source: WHO, FAO Guidelines on Antimicrobial Use*

### Categories of Drugs
- **Antibiotics**: Tetracyclines (e.g., Oxytetracycline), Macrolides (Tylosin), Penicillins (Amoxicillin), Fluoroquinolones (Enrofloxacin - restricted).
- **Anticoccidials**: Ionophores, Chemical.
- **Anthelmintics**: Dewormers.

### Withdrawal Periods [CRITICAL]
- The time required after administering a drug before meat or eggs can be harvested for human consumption to avoid residues.
- **Example**: Amoxicillin in broilers (Meat: 3-7 days depending on jurisdiction).
- **Rule (BR-HLT-03)**: System must lock flock harvesting/egg selling until withdrawal period expires.

### Medication Recording (FR-HLT-03)
- Track: Drug name, active ingredient, dosage, route (water/feed), duration, withdrawal period, veterinary prescription reference.

### Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)
- **Recommendation**: Integrate sensitivity testing results before approving antibiotic usage in the system to ensure judicious use.

## 4. Mortality Analysis

### Mortality Reason Categories
- Disease/Infection, Sudden Death, Culling (leg defects, runts), Predation, Environmental (heat stress, smothering), Unknown.

### Post-Mortem Recording (FR-HLT-04)
- Form to capture gross lesions observed during necropsy by vet/manager.
- **Fields**: Organ systems (Respiratory, Digestive, Skeletal), Lesion description, Presumptive diagnosis.

### Normal vs. Abnormal Thresholds
- **Broilers**: Standard cumulative mortality < 4% over 42 days. Daily normal < 0.1%.
- **Layers/Breeders**: Standard cumulative mortality < 5% during rearing, < 0.5% per month during lay.

## 5. Biosecurity
*Source: FAO Biosecurity for Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza*

### Farm-Level Measures
- **Perimeter Control**: Fencing, single entry point.
- **Visitor Management**: Logbook, mandatory shower-in/shower-out, provided farm clothing/footwear.
- **Vehicle Disinfection**: Wheel dips, undercarriage spraying.
- **All-in, All-out (AIAO)**: Depopulating the entire farm/shed simultaneously to break disease cycles. Minimum 14-day downtime for cleaning and disinfection.
- **Pest Control**: Rodent and wild bird exclusion.

### Biosecurity Audit Checklist (FR-HLT-05)
System module for regular audits:
- [ ] Footbaths maintained at all shed entries?
- [ ] Visitor log up to date?
- [ ] No other poultry/birds kept by staff at home?
- [ ] Feed bins sealed and clean?
- [ ] Dead bird disposal (incinerator/compost) functioning properly?

## 6. Veterinary Records
- **FR-HLT-06**: Vet Visit Logging. Capture date, vet name, observations, recommendations.
- **FR-HLT-07**: Lab Test Results. Upload serology (ELISA titers), PCR results, bacterial culture, and sensitivity.

## 7. Health Alerts System

### Triggers & Severity
- **Critical (Level 1)**: High sudden mortality (e.g., >0.5% in a day), suspected notifiable disease (AI, ND).
  - *Action*: SMS/Email to Farm Owner, Corporate Vet. Immediate quarantine lock on farm in ERP.
- **High (Level 2)**: Significant drop in feed/water consumption (>10%), egg production drop (>5% in a week).
  - *Action*: Alert to Farm Manager and Vet.
- **Medium (Level 3)**: Missed vaccination, impending withdrawal period expiration.
  - *Action*: Dashboard notification to Farm Supervisor.
